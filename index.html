Kenya Land Pressure Atlas

Where Kenya's communities have pushed, been pushed, or been held in place, decade by decade since 1900. Each arrow summarises documented migration and settlement pressure for part of a community, with the main reason behind it. Inward rings mean pressure inside a home area (eviction back home, confinement, crowding); outward rings mean pressure in several directions at once.

    How to read this

    • An arrow is a summary of what some families in a community did, not a claim about the whole community. Kenyans of every background have moved, bought land, been evicted and settled elsewhere.
    • Most pressure came from outside forces: colonial land alienation, state settlement schemes, the land market, population growth, drought and political violence.
    • Much of the post-1963 movement was lawful purchase or allocation. Many of the inward rings record people who lost land or were displaced.
    • Kalenjin sub-groups other than the Kipsigis and Pokot (Nandi, Keiyo, Marakwet, Tugen, Sabaot) are grouped under Nandi and other Kalenjin. Meru, Embu and Tharaka are grouped together, as are Borana and Gabra, and Orma and Pokomo.
